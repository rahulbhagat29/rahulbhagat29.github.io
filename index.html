LendingClub Loan Risk & Portfolio Profit Analysis

Finance / Lending  |  2026

Decision: A 15% default-rate cut-off returns the highest net profit of the thresholds tested — $10.13M, approving 65.5% of segments at an 8.4% portfolio default rate. Risk-based pricing at 1.5× converts Grade C/D/E High-DTI segments from loss to profit. Grade F/G stay loss-making at every pricing level tested and should be excluded from any approval policy.
Caveat: 15% is the tightest threshold simulated, so the optimum is not yet bracketed — tighter cut-offs still need testing.

Problem: Portfolio had no quantified approval policy — approving broadly increased defaults, approving conservatively left profitable loans rejected. No threshold existed to define where approval becomes profit-negative.

Data: LendingClub public loan dataset (Kaggle) — 2,260,701 records, 151 columns, 2007–2018. Analysis runs on a random 50,000-row sample, 49,957 rows after cleaning.

What I did: Cleaned the sample in Python and Pandas (null-column drop, core-field filtering, median imputation on DTI and revolving utilisation, loan-to-income cap), ran multi-segment SQL analysis using a three-CTE master query across risk grades and DTI bands, and built a Python policy simulation sweeping approval thresholds and a 1.5× risk-based pricing scenario.

FinEdge Financial Performance & Profitability Analysis

Finance / SaaS Domain  |  2026

Decision: Marketing overspent budget by 32%, Operations by 14% — enabled direct budget reallocation recommendation before the next fiscal cycle. 39% revenue decline confirmed as market-level correction, not internal failure. Operating Margin held at 24.5% within SaaS benchmark.

Data: Simulated B2B SaaS fintech dataset — 14,280 transactions after cleaning, FY2022–FY2024.

Problem: Finance dataset had 9 data quality issues across 14,280 rows blocking any reliable reporting — including inconsistent department names, mixed date formats, and unclassified transactions.

What I did: Cleaned all data issues in Power Query and built 17 DAX measures from scratch covering Operating Margin %, Variance %, Revenue per Employee, and Recurring Revenue %.

Corporate Budget Variance Analysis & Stress Test Model

Finance / Corporate  |  2026

Decision: Product B and Operations together drive the large majority of adverse variance — enabling targeted corrective action over broad cost cuts. Under severe stress (−12% revenue, +8% cost) Net P&L falls from ₹765L to ₹276L, a 64% erosion; the business stays profitable across the full modelled grid, with the worst case (−15% revenue, +12% cost) still returning ₹114L.

Data: Synthetic 192-row panel (8 line items × 24 months) modelled on mid-market Indian manufacturing benchmarks, FY2023–FY2024.

Problem: The modelled business had no stress-tested view of P&L exposure — management could not quantify how much margin erosion a combined revenue shortfall and cost overrun would produce, making budget defence and scenario planning guesswork.

What I did: Built a 6-sheet financial model covering FY2023–FY2024 across 8 line items and 191 cleaned data rows with 2,266 formulas, zero errors. Engineered a Variance Engine with RAG status flags and a 6×5 sensitivity table (30 scenarios) to stress test Net P&L across revenue and cost combinations.
